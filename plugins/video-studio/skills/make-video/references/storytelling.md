# Storytelling for short vertical video

## Pick a shape
| Goal | Shape | Beats (35 s example) |
|---|---|---|
| Sell a product | Disaster -> fix -> payoff -> offer | hook character + promise (4 s), the problem going wrong (5 s), the turn: where the fix comes from (4 s), 3-5 features as quick fixes (2-3 s each), payoff mirrors the problem (3 s), offer/end card (6-8 s) |
| Explain a topic | Question -> layers -> twist -> takeaway | surprising hook (3 s), what it is (6 s), how it works in 3 steps (15-20 s), the thing people get wrong (8 s), one-line takeaway (4 s) |
| Tell a story or history | Cold open -> rewind -> rise -> fall -> lesson | the dramatic moment (4 s), "it started with..." (6 s), the rise with numbers (10 s), the fall (10 s), what it means now (6 s) |
| How-to | Result first -> steps -> common mistake -> result | finished result (3 s), steps one per scene (4-6 s each), the mistake to avoid (5 s), result again (3 s) |

## Hooks (write three, pick the strongest)
- A person in trouble: "This is Dave. Dave invited twelve friends over. On this grill."
- A number that surprises: "In 2000 this company was worth $125 billion. A year later it was gone."
- A thing everyone ignores: "This is a driveway. You've never thought about it once."
- A direct promise for the viewer: "Here's why your burgers burn."
Avoid: "In this video...", greetings, logos first, questions with obvious answers.

## Writing the lines
- Budget: seconds x 2.6 words. A 35 s ad holds about 85-90 words.
- One line = one breath, 3-12 words. Each line should give the animation something to show.
- Humor: understatement, a running gag that pays off at the end ("zero smoke alarms"), specific details
  ("charcoal, with a hint of burger"). Never mock the audience or the client's competitors by name.
- Numbers as words in script.txt ("two hundred twenty-five") so the voice reads them right; map them back to
  digits for captions with build(caption_text={"two hundred twenty-five": "225"}).
- Call to action: what, how much, where. "The Char-Broil grill. Now just eighty-nine ninety-nine. Get yours at
  Busy Beaver."

## Research
- 3-6 solid sources; open them. Prefer primary sources (the maker, government, the company's own pages).
- Facts table in research.md: fact, number, source link, date. Use only what the video needs.
- For "how to" topics, confirm the audience version (home vs commercial, beginner vs pro, country).

## Visual beats
For each line decide what appears on which word: the noun appears when it is said, the number counts up when it
is said, the stamp lands on the punchline word. A scene with nothing moving for over 1.5 s feels dead: add a
drift, a bob, a wobble or a label.
